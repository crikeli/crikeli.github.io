About

I live in Seattle with my wife and our cat, Bucket. I'm a software engineer who's ended up pulled toward geospatial work — not because maps are novel, but because local GIS data is one of the most direct ways to actually see how a city or county is functioning, and where it isn't.

I think GIS has the potential for genuinely profound insight into how communities work: where resources are under- or over-allocated, where an official designation doesn't match what's really on the ground, where a dataset everyone relies on has quietly gone stale. The KC Data Quality Audit and Seattle Urban Forest Wildfire Risk projects below both grew out of that belief — one checks whether King County's own public datasets can be trusted, the other checks whether the county's wildfire risk designation actually covers the places that look risky on the ground.

I like working with satellite and aerial imagery through traditional computer vision workflows, and I like just as much getting my hands into messy, real-world data and making sense of it — parcel records, assessor photos, crime reports, whatever a county or city actually publishes.

I'm also a proponent of agentic software development — with real guardrails, not blind delegation. AI can speed up how fast you build something, but it shouldn't replace understanding what you built.
